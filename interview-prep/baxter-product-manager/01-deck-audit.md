# 01. Deck Audit: what they will poke, what's missing, how to patch it

Deck: *SUPRANE® 2026 Business Strategy* (12 slides).
Brief asked for: market analysis and segmentation, target audience, marketing strategy, budget and ROI, performance metrics.

**Verdict:** The structure is strong. Issue tree, 2x2 segmentation, 3 imperatives, KPI tables, and a quarterly roadmap. That reads like a manager. The risk is the **numbers**. Three of them won't survive a finance-minded panelist, and the panel will find them in the first 10 minutes. Fix them before they do, or own them out loud.

## Slide by slide

### Slide 1. Title
- **Fine.** Add a one-line promise under the title if you can: *"From -40% to $1.5M run-rate: fix access, focus users, protect the licence to operate."* It tells them where the next 15 minutes go.

### Slide 2. Sales and issue tree
- **Good:** It separates *market contraction* (-35%) from *Suprane underperformance* (-40%). That's senior thinking. Lead with it out loud.
- **They will ask:** "Why did a whole inhaled-anesthesia market drop 35% in one year?" Surgical volumes don't fall 35% in a year.
- **Missing:** Evidence for each branch of the issue tree. Right now they are hypotheses.
- **How to answer:** "It's outside-in data. A 35% drop in a procedure-driven category is unusual, so before I believed it I'd check sell-in versus sell-out, tender timing, distributor stock, and any supply interruptions. Baxter reported inhaled-anesthesia declines and supply constraints in 2026, so some of this may be supply, not demand. That changes the fix."
- **Power move:** Say "I'd love to hear what you saw internally." They know the real reason. Asking shows you want truth over defending your slide.

### Slide 3. Market analysis
- **Good:** Sevo / iso / des comparison. Clear "don't fight sevo everywhere" logic in the notes.
- **They will ask:** "Where does the share data come from?" (You wrote *based on assumption*.)
- **Missing:** Market size in value and volume, number of ORs or surgeries, vaporizer installed base, pricing by brand, same-molecule desflurane competitors.
- **Answer:** Name the sources you'd use: distributor sell-out, hospital purchase data, tender results, field census of vaporizers through reps and biomedical engineering, third-party hospital audit where available. Q1 "validate data" is already on your roadmap. Point to it.

### Slide 4. SWOT
- **Good:** Balanced. ERAS and partner-led access are the right opportunities.
- **They will ask:** "Which one item in the SWOT matters most?" Have a single answer: **the vaporizer gap**. It's the only barrier that blocks use entirely. Everything else just slows it.
- **Missing:** Supply reliability (Baxter's 2026 supply constraints), JKN/BPJS cost pressure, compliance limits on equipment placement.

### Slide 5. Objective
- **The weakest slide.** See fix #1 and #2. Rehearse the 20-second patch until it sounds calm.
- **Add verbally:** bear / base / bull. See `04-numbers-cheat-sheet.md`.

### Slide 6. Segmentation 2x2
- **Good:** Potential x readiness is the right lens for a capital-constrained product.
- **They will ask:** "How many hospitals are in each box? How much revenue sits there?"
- **Missing:** Counts, revenue per quadrant, and the cut-off definitions ("high surgical volume" = how many cases a month?).
- **Answer:** "I didn't quantify the boxes, and that's the first thing I'd do in Q1. Hospital list by class and OR count, vaporizer census from reps and biomed, 12 months of consumption. Four weeks of work. My working hypothesis: 80% of revenue sits in fewer than 30 accounts." (Say *hypothesis*.)

### Slide 7. Stakeholders
- **Good:** 4 groups (clinical, operational, access, executive) is right.
- **Missing:** What each one *needs to hear*, their objection, and who decides. This is the "target audience identification" part of the brief. It's currently a list, not a strategy.
- **Have this ready:**

| Stakeholder | What they care about | Their objection | Our message |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anesthesiologist | Emergence, airway safety, control | "Sevo works fine" | Faster, more predictable wake-up in long, obese, elderly cases |
| OR manager / PACU | Turnover, PACU time | "Changing agents disrupts workflow" | Shorter PACU stay frees beds and OR slots |
| Biomed engineering | Equipment, maintenance | "We don't have the vaporizer" | We install, train, and service it |
| Procurement / pharmacy | Price per bottle, tender | "Too expensive" | Compare cost per case at low flow, not price per bottle |
| Hospital management | Throughput, governance, sustainability | "Climate concerns" | Responsible-use protocol plus low-flow, used only where it adds value |

### Slide 8. Strategic imperatives
- **Good:** Accelerate / Protect / Unlock with a segment per pillar. Patient focus (>2h, obese, >65) is sharp and clinical.
- **They will ask:** "If you could only fund one, which?" Answer: **PROTECT first**, because it defends the $0.9M base for $5k. Then ACCELERATE (highest certainty). UNLOCK is the biggest bet, so it gets a pilot and a go/no-go.

### Slides 9-10. Initiatives
- **Good:** KPI, owner, timing, budget, impact per line. Named programs (ASCEND, PROVE) feel real.
- **They will ask:**
  - "How did you get $360k?" (6 vaporizers x 20 bottles x 12 months x ~$250/bottle.)
  - "Is 20 bottles per vaporizer a month realistic?" (See cheat sheet. It implies heavy daily use.)
  - "+20 active users from 3 events. Show me the funnel."
  - "PROVE has zero sales impact. Why spend $8k?" (It creates the local evidence that ASCEND and sales use in Q3-Q4. It's an enabler.)
  - "Isn't the KSO/leasing program a compliance risk?" (See question bank.)
  - "Who pays for the vaporizers? Is $25k enough?" Know your assumption. If Baxter buys units, that's placed equipment or capex, separate from the A&P budget. Have payback per site ready: ~$5k revenue a month per site at target use.
- **Missing:** Digital or omnichannel (webinars, e-detailing, accredited CME). Pricing and tender strategy. A distributor plan beyond "joint business plan".

### Slide 11. Roadmap
- **Good:** "Go/no-go to scale beyond Jakarta" and "mid-year review, reallocate budget" are manager-grade signals. **Point at them during the talk.** They show you know when to stop.

### Slide 12. Thank you
- Replace with, or add before it, **a one-slide summary**: problem, 3 moves, $50k, $1.5M run-rate, decision you need from them. End on the ask, not on "thank you".

## Missing at manager level (they'll test these even if the brief didn't ask)
1. **Why Suprane** over C and O, and a 60-second view on each. (Question bank, round G.)
2. **Lifecycle honesty.** Desflurane is restricted in the EU from 1 Jan 2026 except where medically needed. Is this a growth product or a disciplined harvest? Have a view.
3. **Supply.** Baxter reported 2026 supply constraints in injectables and anesthesia. Don't build demand you can't fill. Product C's case shows what a stock-out does.
4. **Indonesia access reality.** JKN/BPJS bundled payments (INA-CBG) squeeze cost per case. Check Fornas and e-katalog listing status for public hospitals.
5. **Compliance.** Equipment placed in exchange for volume commitments needs fair-market-value structuring and legal sign-off. You have legal review in Q1. Say so.
6. **Portfolio and cannibalisation.** If Baxter Indonesia also sells sevoflurane, the account story is "right agent for the right patient", and Baxter wins either way.
7. **3-year view.** 2026 stabilise and prove. 2027 scale or stop. 2028 hold a profitable clinical niche.
