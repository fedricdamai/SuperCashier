## Slide 2. Sales and issue tree

**1. "Why did the whole market drop 35% in one year?"**
> "Honestly, I don't fully believe it yet. Surgeries didn't fall 35%. My data is outside-in, so first I'd check sell-in versus sell-out, tender timing, distributor stock, and supply. Baxter reported inhaled-anesthesia declines and supply constraints this year, so part of it may be supply, not demand. What did you see internally?"

**2. "Can you really claim 'the fastest recovery'?"**
> "Desflurane has the lowest blood-gas solubility of the modern volatile agents, which is why emergence is fastest. In promotion I'd keep to what Medical and Regulatory approve, and focus where the evidence is strongest: long cases, obese and elderly patients."

## Slide 3. Market analysis

**3. "Your share data says 'based on assumption'. What would you actually use?"**
> "Distributor sell-out by account, hospital purchase data, tender results, and a vaporizer census by reps and biomedical engineering. That's the first item on my Q1 roadmap: validate the data."

**4. "Why not just convert sevoflurane users? They're 66% of the market."**
> "Sevo is a good, cheaper, versatile drug. A head-on fight loses. I want the cases where speed of wake-up changes something: over two hours, obese, over 65. A small slice of sevo's 66% in those cases is enough."

## Slide 4. SWOT

**5. "Which single item in your SWOT matters most?"**
> "The vaporizer. It's the only barrier that stops use completely. Every other weakness slows us down. This one shuts the door."

## Slide 5. Objective

**6. "$1.7M at 35% share means the market has to grow 35%. Why would it?"**
> "Yes, it assumes the market recovers to about 4.9 million. That's still 13% below 2024. I think part of the 2025 drop was temporary: supply, tender timing, stock. Q1 data validation will confirm it. And 35% share is my real anchor. If the market stays flat, 35% is about 1.3 million, which is still +44%."

**7. "Your programs start in Q2 and Q3. How do they deliver full-year numbers?"**
> "The bridge shows each program at full speed. In-year, they add about 0.4 million. The rest comes from the market: 25% of a 4.9 million market is 1.2 million on its own. Both roads land near 1.7. If installs slip or the market doesn't recover, we land around 1.3. So the dates I'd manage hardest are account plans by March, ASCEND Jakarta by May, all vaporizers by June."

**8. "+89%? Your best year ever was $1.5M. Isn't that unrealistic?"**
> "It's ambitious by design. 2024 showed this brand can grow 50% in a year. A PM should set a target that stretches the team, and know exactly what has to be true for it. Here that's the market recovery and three dates. And I know my floor: about 1.3 million at the same 35% share."

## Slide 6. Segmentation

**9. "How did you define 'high potential' and 'high readiness'?"**
> "Potential is surgical volume: general-anesthesia cases a month, with cut-offs agreed with sales. Readiness is whether a desflurane vaporizer is there and whether active users exist. In total that's 63 target accounts: 45 base and reactivated in WIN and DEFEND, 10 new through ASCEND, and 8 new through vaporizer placement in UNLOCK."

## Slides 7 and 8. Stakeholders and imperatives

**10. "Who's the real decision maker?"**
> "The anesthesiologist picks the agent case by case, but three gates come first. Procurement lists it. Biomed decides on the vaporizer. Management sets sustainability policy. So four messages: wake-up and airway safety for clinicians, PACU time for the OR manager, cost per case for procurement, responsible use for management."

**11. "If you could fund only one pillar, which one?"**
> "PROTECT first. $3,000 guards $0.12 million inside the base. Then ACCELERATE, because it has the highest certainty. UNLOCK is the biggest bet, so it gets a pilot and a go/no-go."

## Slide 9. ACCELERATE initiatives

**12. "The accelerator costs $2k and shows $1.16M impact. Really?"**
> "That $1.16 million includes the base we keep. The new money is $264k on a run-rate basis, about $200k in 2026. And the real cost isn't the $2,000. It's rep time, which we redirect from low-value calls to the accounts that matter."

**13. "What's the key message for ASCEND, and how do 3 events give you 20 new users?"**
> "The campaign is 'Own the Wake-Up': in long cases, obese patients and the over-65s, wake-up is the riskiest part of the anesthetic, and Suprane gives it back faster. 150 invited, 90 attend, and a rep follows up within two weeks with a 'first five cases' kit: patient-selection card, low-flow guide, PACU tracking sheet. If a quarter convert, that's about 20 new and 15 expanding. The event opens the door. The follow-up converts."

**14. "What if PROVE shows Suprane adds nothing?"**
> "Then we've learned it for $5,000. Medical leads it, the endpoints are agreed before we start, and results are shared either way. If Suprane doesn't help in a patient group, I stop promoting it there and move the money."

## Slide 10. UNLOCK and PROTECT

**15. "Placing vaporizers for volume commitments. Isn't that a compliance risk?"**
> "It can be, which is why legal and compliance review is the first step in Q1. The agreement is with the hospital, never a doctor, at fair market value, documented, and checked against Baxter's code and local industry codes. If compliance says no, I'd move to a distributor rental model rather than cut corners."

**16. "Is $30k enough for 8 vaporizers? What's the payback?"**
> "Each site at 10 bottles a month brings about $2,500 a month in revenue. If the units are bought rather than leased, that's capex, separate from my marketing budget, and I'd confirm unit cost with the distributor in Q1. Either way, at target usage a site pays back within the year."

**17. "What if vaporizers get installed and nobody uses them?"**
> "That's my biggest risk. So: only high-volume ORs, a signed usage commitment, training before install, a 3-month usage check, and a rule agreed up front. Under 5 bottles a month after three months, we move the vaporizer."

**18. "Your low-flow program cuts bottles per case. Aren't you hurting your own revenue?"**
> "Per case, yes, on purpose. A hospital that bans Suprane costs me 100% of that account. Low-flow keeps us in hospitals that care about emissions and makes cost per case competitive with sevo. Growth comes from more patients and more users, not more waste."

**19. "Europe restricted desflurane in 2026. Why invest in a product the world is phasing out?"**
> "Even the EU keeps it where it's medically needed, and that's the same principle I'm using: right patient, low flow, never the default. Indonesia has no restriction today. And there's a stop rule: if sustainability policy here moves faster, risk mapping tells us early, and we manage Suprane for profit instead of chasing growth."

> "It's volume those high-risk accounts cut in 2025 over environmental concerns. That's the perception branch of my issue tree. Stewardship wins it back, so it's recovery, not money we already have. Without PROTECT, those accounts keep shrinking."

**21. "What's the ROI?"**
> "With the full plan, 50 thousand drives about 0.8 million of new revenue: 16 times revenue, about 8.6 times gross margin at an assumed 60%. Even in a flat market it's about 0.4 million new, so roughly 3.8 times gross margin. The real constraint isn't money. It's field time and vaporizers."

## Slide 12. Roadmap and governance

**22. "It's June. How do I know this is working?"**
> "Revenue will lag, so I'd watch leading indicators. Inactive users reactivated. Bottles per active user from sell-out. Vaporizers installed and bottles per vaporizer each month. High-risk accounts engaged. One monthly dashboard, and a mid-year review where budget moves to what's working."

**23. "I'm cutting your budget in half. What stays?"**
> "Protect the base, fund the certain, shrink the bet. Stewardship 3k and the accelerator 2k stay whole. The vaporizer pilot drops to 4 sites, about 15k, still enough for a go/no-go. ASCEND becomes Jakarta only. PROVE runs in one hospital. About 25k in total."

## Strategic and portfolio

**24. "Why Suprane, not C or O?"**
> "C leads with 67% share. Its job is to defend without a price war. O is flat, held back by central-only access. Suprane lost 40% in a year. That's where a product manager changes the outcome most. I have a short view on C and O if useful."

**25. "One budget for all three products. How do you split it?"**
> "Each gets a role. C is three-quarters of revenue: defend it, so it gets about half, spent on supply reliability and tenders, not price. Suprane is a turnaround with a gate. O builds through C, because parenteral nutrition needs daily vitamins, so I'd bundle them. Roughly half, a quarter, a quarter, then move money at mid-year."

**26. "Baxter has had supply constraints in anesthesia this year. What if we can't supply?"**
> "Then demand programs wait for supply. I'd align ASCEND and the installs with supply planning before launch. Product C shows why: the case lists its last supply issue as a challenge. A stock-out after an event burns trust that took a year to build."

**27. "What's your three-year view?"**
> "2026: $1.7 million at 35% share, and a proven access model in Jakarta. 2027: $2 million-plus if the Q4 gate says scale, with 8 more vaporizers in Surabaya and Bandung and a second ASCEND wave. 2028: Suprane as a premium agent for defined patients, low-flow as standard. If policy moves faster, we manage it for profit and shift investment to nutrition."

**28. "The sales team carries many products. Why would they make time for Suprane?"**
> "Because I'll make it small, clear and worth it. A list of target accounts with a plan each, not 'push it everywhere'. A Suprane line in incentives agreed with the sales head. Wins shown in the monthly review."

**29. "Your background is oncology. What transfers, and what's new?"**
> "What transfers: hospital selling with several decision makers (doctors, pharmacy, procurement, formulary), evidence-led promotion, KOL work, tenders. What's new is the OR itself: equipment, workflow, and how anesthesiologists choose agents case by case. I'd close that in my first month by riding with reps, observing in the OR with a host hospital, and spending time with biomed and medical."

**30. "Biggest weakness of your plan?"**
> "My data is outside-in. Segment sizes and market numbers need validation, which is why Q1 is 'prepare', not 'launch'. If the data says the 2025 drop was mostly supply, I'd shift money from ASCEND into winning back the accounts we lost."
