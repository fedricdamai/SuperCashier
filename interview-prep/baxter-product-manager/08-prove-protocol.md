# 08. PROVE Protocol Design

**PROVE** = Practice & Real-world OR Validation & Evidence
**Budget:** $5,000 · **Sites:** 2 Jakarta hospitals · **Timeline:** Q1 to Q2 · **Owner:** Medical Affairs (lead), Marketing (funding and use of results)

## The one-line pitch
> "PROVE measures what fast wake-up actually looks like in Indonesian operating rooms, so ASCEND can sell with local proof instead of global slides."

---

## 1. Why PROVE exists
- Anesthesiologists trust **local** data more than global studies.
- ASCEND needs real Indonesian cases to show at events.
- It also checks your own claims. If Suprane doesn't help in a patient group, you stop promoting it there.

## 2. Design
| Item | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Type | **Prospective, observational, non-interventional** | The doctor chooses the agent as usual. Baxter doesn't influence treatment. |
| Comparison | Suprane cases vs sevoflurane cases in the same hospitals | Gives context without a costly randomized trial |
| Sites | 2 Jakarta hospitals with vaporizers and high surgical volume | Picked by Medical on scientific criteria, **not by sales volume** |
| Patients | Adults, general anesthesia, inhaled maintenance | Desflurane is used for maintenance in adults, not inhaled induction |
| Focus groups | Surgery over 2 hours · BMI 30 or more · age 65 or more | Your three target patient groups |
| Exclude | Emergency surgery, patients going to ICU still intubated, standard contraindications | Keeps emergence data clean |
| Sample | **About 120 cases** (60 Suprane, 60 sevo) | Enough for a credible local picture. Descriptive, not a registration trial. |
| Duration | About 10 weeks of data collection | 2 sites × ~6 cases a week each |

## 3. What we measure (endpoints)

**Primary**
- **Time to extubation:** minutes from vaporizer off to breathing tube removed

**Secondary: clinical**
- Time to eye opening and to responding to commands
- Airway events at emergence: oxygen saturation under 90%, laryngospasm, re-intubation
- Nausea and vomiting in recovery (PONV)

**Secondary: workflow (what hospital management cares about)**
- Time in recovery (PACU) until discharge criteria met (modified Aldrete score of 9 or more)
- Time from end of surgery to leaving the OR

**Secondary: responsible use (links to PROTECT)**
- Fresh gas flow used during maintenance (L/min)
- Agent used per hour
- Estimated CO2-equivalent per case

**Satisfaction**
- Anesthesiologist rating of emergence quality, 1 to 5

## 4. Governance and compliance
- **Medical Affairs is the sponsor.** Marketing funds it and uses approved results. Marketing does not design conclusions.
- **Ethics approval** from each hospital's ethics committee before the first patient. Consent as the committee requires.
- **No patient identities reach Baxter.** Data is anonymized at the site.
- **Not a seeding study.** Sites aren't chosen for sales potential, and no purchase is linked to taking part. Fees to sites are fair market value for real data-collection work.
- **Results shared whatever they show.**

## 7. How the results get used
1. **ASCEND:** Medical presents local results as scientific exchange.
2. **"First five cases" kit:** sharpen the patient-selection card with what the data shows.
3. **Stewardship:** set a low-flow benchmark from real Indonesian practice.
4. **Go/no-go:** if Suprane shows no benefit in a group, remove that group from promotion.

## 8. Better KPIs (replace "Protocol completed 100%")
"Protocol completed" counts activity. These measure progress:
- Ethics approval by **end of February**
- **120 cases** collected by **mid-May**
- Report finished by **end of June**
- Results presented at **2 ASCEND events** in Q3

## 9. Risks
| Risk | Fix |
|---|---|
| Ethics approval is slow | Submit in January. Choose sites with a fast ethics track record. |
| Too few cases | Add a third site or extend 4 weeks |
| Bias: doctors pick Suprane for certain patients | Compare within the same patient groups (long, obese, elderly) and present it as descriptive data |
| Results are weak | Share them anyway. Narrow promotion to where Suprane helps. |

## Likely panel questions

**"Is this a real study or a marketing exercise?"**
> "It's a non-interventional study led by Medical, with ethics approval at each site. Marketing funds it, but the endpoints are set before we start and the results are shared whatever they show."

**"Isn't paying hospitals to use Suprane in a study a seeding trial?"**
> "That's exactly what I'd avoid. Doctors choose the agent as they normally would. Sites are picked on scientific criteria, not sales. Fees are fair market value for data collection, and no purchase is linked."

**"Why only 120 patients? That proves nothing."**
> "It's not meant to replace published trials. Those already show faster emergence. It answers a local question: does that difference hold in our ORs, in our patients? For a $5,000 study, that's the right size."

**"What if Suprane doesn't beat sevo?"**
> "Then we've learned it for $5,000 instead of spending $40,000 promoting in the wrong place. I'd narrow the patient groups and move the money."

**"Why measure CO2 per case?"**
> "Because responsible use needs a baseline. If we promote low-flow, we should show how much it cuts emissions in real practice."
