# 03. Full Mock Interview: 90 minutes, played straight

**Slot:** tomorrow, 12:00 to 13:30. Online. 6 panelists plus the organizer.
**Likely shape:** 5 min intro, 20 min presentation, 50 min Q&A, 10 min your questions, 5 min close.

**How to run it tonight:** get a friend to read the interviewer lines, or record yourself on your phone and play the interviewer. Stand up. Keep a 2-minute timer for each answer. Don't read the "What good sounds like" notes until you've answered.

Panel personas used below (match them to real titles once you've checked LinkedIn, see `06-panel-and-closing.md`):
- **HM** = hiring manager / commercial head. Practical. Cares about execution and the sales team.
- **RM** = regional marketing. Cares about strategy, consistency and the global story.
- **FIN** = finance-minded leader. Checks every number.
- **MED** = medical affairs. Cares about evidence and claims.
- **HR** = HR / talent. Cares about leadership, motivation and fit.

---

## Part 1. Warm-up (0:00 to 0:05)

**HR:** "Thanks for joining. Before the presentation, give us a quick introduction. Two minutes."

> **What good sounds like:** Present, past, why here. One proof point with a number. End by pointing to the case: *"...which is why I enjoyed this case. It's exactly the kind of problem I want to own."* No life story.

---

## Part 2. Presentation (0:05 to 0:25)

Target: **18 minutes** for 12 slides. Slides 5, 6, 8 and 9-10 get the most time.

**Your opening line (memorize):**
> "Suprane lost 40% of its sales last year. Most of that was a shrinking market. The rest was us. Today I'll show you why, where to play, and how 50 thousand dollars gets us back to a 1.5 million run-rate by the end of 2026."

**Pre-empt the two weak spots inside the talk.** Don't wait for the questions:
- On slide 2: *"These numbers are outside-in. A 35% market drop is unusual, so the first thing I'd do in the job is check sell-in versus sell-out."*
- On slide 5: *"1.5 million at 35% assumes the market recovers about 19%. If it doesn't, I commit to revenue, not share. I'll show the scenarios in Q&A."*

**Closing line (memorize):**
> "Protect the base, accelerate where we're ready, unlock where we're not. 50 thousand dollars, a Q3 go/no-go, and a clear rule for when to stop. Happy to go wherever you'd like."

---

## Part 3. Q&A with follow-up chains (0:25 to 1:15)

Senior panels don't ask 20 separate questions. They ask 5 and **drill down 3 levels** until your answer breaks. Practise the chains, not just the first question.

### Chain 1. The target (FIN)
1. "Walk me through how 1.5 million and 35% share fit together."
2. "So which number should I hold you to?"
3. "And if the market drops another 10%?"
4. "What's the number below which you'd tell us to stop investing?"

> **What good sounds like:**
> 1. 1.5 / 0.35 = 4.3M market, a 19% recovery. Flat market means 42%.
> 2. "Revenue. 1.3M in calendar 2026, 1.5M+ run-rate exiting Q4."
> 3. Bear case: market 3.24M, Suprane about 1.0M, 31% share. "We'd still hold share. The plan protects the base first."
> 4. Give a real rule: *"If by the Q3 review the vaporizer sites average under 8 bottles a month and reactivated users haven't moved, I'd recommend managing Suprane for profit and moving the money to nutrition."* A stop rule is what separates a manager from a brand enthusiast.

### Chain 2. The vaporizer program (HM, then FIN)
1. "Tell me more about the KSO program. Who owns the vaporizer?"
2. "Isn't tying equipment to volume a compliance problem?"
3. "Is 20 bottles a month per vaporizer realistic?"
4. "The hospital signs, the vaporizer arrives, nobody uses it. Now what?"

> **What good sounds like:**
> 1. Hospital-level agreement, Baxter or distributor-owned, fair market value, with install, training and service.
> 2. "Yes, it can be. That's why legal and compliance review is step one in Q1. Contract with the hospital, never a doctor. Documented. FMV."
> 3. "It's ambitious. 20 bottles is roughly 150 to 280 anesthesia hours a month. I'd only place units in ORs that can show that volume and set 12 to 15 for the first three months."
> 4. Diagnose before pulling it: training gap? champion left? supply? Then the pre-agreed rule: under 8 bottles after 3 months, redeploy.

### Chain 3. Sustainability (RM, then MED)
1. "Europe restricted desflurane this year. Why should Baxter Indonesia invest in it?"
2. "So you're promoting a high-emission product with a green label. Isn't that greenwashing?"
3. "Your low-flow program cuts bottles per case. You're cutting your own revenue."
4. "Medical says your toolkit claims are too strong. What now?"

> **What good sounds like:**
> 1. Even the EU keeps it for medical need. Same principle: right patient, low-flow, not default. Indonesia has no restriction. The plan has a stop rule.
> 2. **Stay calm. This is a pressure test.** *"It would be if we claimed Suprane is green. We don't. We'd say it has a high climate impact, and here's how to use it responsibly and only where it adds value. Honest beats defensive. Hospitals trust the supplier who tells them the downside."*
> 3. "Yes, deliberately. Losing a hospital to a ban costs 100%. Low-flow costs a few bottles per case and keeps us in the account."
> 4. "Then medical's version goes out. Narrow and true beats broad and risky."

### Chain 4. Root cause (HM)
1. "Why did the whole market fall 35% in 2025?"
2. "If you had one week of data access, what would you look at first?"
3. "Suppose it turns out most of the drop was our own supply problem. Does your plan change?"

> **What good sounds like:**
> 1. "I don't believe it fully yet. Surgeries didn't fall 35%. I'd check sell-in versus sell-out, tender timing, stock, supply."
> 2. "Distributor sell-out by account for 24 months. It tells me if hospitals stopped *using* it or just stopped *buying* it."
> 3. "Yes, a lot. If it's supply, the first move is S&OP and a recovery message to lost accounts, not events. I'd move part of the ASCEND budget into a win-back program for accounts that switched during the stock-out." This shows you hold your plan loosely. Senior panels love that.

### Chain 5. Portfolio (country or regional leader)
1. "Why Suprane, not C?"
2. "Fine. Give me your plan for C in 60 seconds."
3. "If you had one budget for all three, how would you split it?"
4. "Why not just harvest Suprane and put everything into C?"

> **What good sounds like:** see question bank A1, G2, G1, G6. The link to land: **O and C belong together.** Parenteral nutrition protocols call for daily vitamins, so a C + O bundle in nutrition-support accounts helps both.

### Chain 6. Leading through others (HM, then HR)
1. "Sales carries a full bag. Why would they make time for Suprane?"
2. "The sales director disagrees with your account list. What do you do?"
3. "Tell me about a time you had to change someone's mind without authority."

> **What good sounds like:** small list, clear plan, aligned incentive, visible wins. On disagreement: ask why first, then propose a test and let data decide. Then a real STAR story with a number.

### Rapid fire (any panelist, 30 seconds each)
- "What's the one KPI you'd look at every Monday?" → *Bottles per active user from sell-out.*
- "What would you stop doing?" → *Broad promotion in low-potential, no-vaporizer accounts. Any price cut to buy share.*
- "What's missing from your plan?" → *Pricing and tender strategy, and quantified segments. Here's how I'd add them.*
- "One thing you'd change in your deck now?" → *Commit to revenue, show share as an output, and phase the impact by launch date.*
- "What do you know about Baxter right now?" → *New CEO since Aug 2025, focus on operations and balance sheet. Q3 sales $2.98B, +5%. Anesthesia under supply and demand pressure this year.*

Offering a revision shows coachability. Senior hiring managers remember it.

## Scoring rubric (score yourself after the run, 1 to 5)

| Dimension | 5 looks like | Your score |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Answer first, then 2 or 3 reasons. Under 2 minutes. | |
| Numbers | Every claim has a number. Knows the math behind every slide. | |
| Ownership of gaps | Flags weak spots before being asked. No defensiveness. | |
| Strategic altitude | Talks portfolio, lifecycle, stop rules, not just activities. | |
| Indonesia realism | JKN, tenders, distributor, vaporizer reality. | |
| Leadership | Influences sales, medical and distributor without authority. | |
| Presence | Calm under the greenwashing challenge. Pauses. Doesn't ramble. | |

**Anything under 4:** run that chain again tomorrow morning.

## Common failure modes on a senior panel
- Answering the question *behind* the question before the actual one. Answer first.
- Saying "we" so much your role disappears.
- Defending a number you can't support. Revising it calmly scores higher.
- Running out of time on the presentation and rushing slides 9-11. Those are where budget and ROI live.
- Forgetting there are 6 people. Make eye contact with the camera, and name people when you answer them ("Good question, Pak Farid").
